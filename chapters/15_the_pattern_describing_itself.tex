\noindent \textsc{This book is a computable structure.} It is a finite pattern of symbols, so it is somewhere in the Library, at its coordinates, like everything else. It describes the Library, and it sits inside the Library it describes. You are reading it, which is another configuration the Library holds: a pattern with your exact memories and your exact attention, holding this book, at this index, now.

So the book turns, at the end, and looks at itself. It has spent fourteen chapters saying the structure is indifferent. Here is the last thing it can say, and it is not an argument.

Gather the cold first. The measure does not prefer your index; you are one of countless patterns an interpreter would call you, and it singles out none of them. It does not prefer this system; every set of laws is in the Library, and yours is simple, which is why you are in it, and that is the whole of the reason. There is no view from the top: no mind surveys the structure, because the only thing above all minds is the structure, and the structure is not a mind. At the bottom there is no necessary being who meant any of it, only a near-necessary totality that chose nothing and a brute fact it cannot talk its way out of. No one above. No reason beneath. The room is as cold as the book can make it, and the book has not warmed it with anything untrue.

And here you are.

At an index, finite, with this book in your hands and, very likely, people in the next room or the next call or the unanswered message. None of that is handed down. There is no one to hand it down; that was the whole point. The warmth is not in the structure. It is between the rungs, between finite patterns at their coordinates, or it is nowhere. It is the cut we keep making toward each other, the one carving the structure will not make for us.

This is the move the book was for. Care is not something the cosmos provides and you receive. It is something a pattern does, here, where it is, with the small reach an index allows. The structure does not require it. The structure does not reward it. The structure does not notice. And a configuration that holds care is, even so, not the same as one that does not, not because the measure marks the difference but because you do.

Consider the object itself: paper, or a lit screen, a finite arrangement of symbols. It is one configuration the Library holds. Someone, at their index, set these words down with some care, to reach a pattern they would never meet. You, at yours, are reading them. That is all this is, and it is not small. A pattern described itself and chose, in the describing, to be kind about it. The reading is the same choice, made again from the other side.

Rage is the right first response to the cold. Rage at the indifference of the measure, at the brute fact, at the absent overseer; the rage is earned. It also passes. What is left when it passes is not despair, and it is not comfort. It is a finite pattern, at its coordinates, deciding what kind of configuration to be, with the time the index allows.

Camus, at the end of his own cold argument, wrote \emph{il faut imaginer Sisyphe heureux}: one must imagine Sisyphus happy. The \emph{must} is not a derivation. The structure permits the happiness and the despair with the same indifference. It is a commitment, made without the cosmos's permission, because no permission was ever coming. The author wrote this book as that commitment, performed. You, reading it with care, are the same commitment, performed again. There is no one above to see it. We see it.

Is it enough? I do not know. I cannot reach the indices where the tending failed, and I cannot promise that the braid you can hold answers the ones you cannot. I hope it does. The hope is not a proof, and I will not hand it to you as one. But the choosing does not wait on the answer. We do it unsure, which is the only way it was ever going to be done.
