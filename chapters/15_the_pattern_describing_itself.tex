\chapter[The Pattern Describing Itself]{The Pattern\\Describing Itself}

\chapterepigraph[0.6]
{The growing good of the world is partly dependent on unhistoric acts; and that things are not so ill with you and me as they might have been, is half owing to the number who lived faithfully a hidden life, and rest in unvisited tombs.}
{George Eliot, \textit{Middlemarch} (1872)}

\noindent \textsc{This book is a finite pattern of symbols.} A short program could print it, so it is one of the Library's describable outputs, along with every misprint of itself. Whatever else that means, and the book has kept that question open, this copy is here, and you are reading it now.

So the book turns, at the end, and looks at itself. It has spent fourteen chapters saying the structure is indifferent. Here is the last thing it can say, and it is not an argument.

Gather the cold first. The measure ranks programs; it does not rank you, and if many patterns match you, it cannot say which one you are. Nothing marks this system as the real one, and the book has not been able to tell you whether the Library exists at all; that the question stays open is part of the cold. Every view is a cut. Underneath, nothing in the framework says why there is anything. No view from above. No reason beneath. The room is as cold as the book can honestly make it, and it has not been warmed with anything untrue.

And here you are.

In a local situation, finite, with this book in your hands and, somewhere within reach, a next room, a next call, an unanswered message. No global measure turns those relations into obligations for you. Their warmth is made between finite people through attention and action. It is the cut we keep making toward each other, the one the formalism will not make for us.

This is the move the book was for. Care is not a conclusion the cosmology provides. It is something a person does here, with limited causal reach. The structure does not require it. The structure does not reward it. The structure does not notice. And still, a table where someone stayed a little past when they meant to leave is not the same table, for the two people at it, as one where they got up and went. The difference is small and local, and it is the only one this book has ever been able to point to.

Rage at this is appropriate: at the questions that stay open, at the facts that sit there unexplained, at a structure with no rescue written into it. Let it come. It passes, or it did for me, mostly. If yours has not, nothing here asks you to hurry it. What comes after is smaller than comfort and steadier than despair. Call it equanimity, if the word can be made that small. And care is still there, because it was never coming from the structure in the first place.

\textit{Worldlines} came to this place from a warmer room. Its geometry was indifferent, but it was one geometry, and your worldline was one definite curve inside it; care could stand out as a bright figure against a dark ground. This book could not give you that ground. Here even the line around the figure is one you draw. The care is the same care. It has nothing under it, and it is held anyway.

Consider the object itself: paper, or a lit screen, a finite arrangement of symbols. Someone set these words down with some care, to reach a reader they might never meet. You are reading them. That is all this is, and it is not small. A person described a pattern and chose, in the describing, to be kind about it. Reading carefully can answer from the other side.

That is what the preface meant by \emph{fellow sufferer}. Not a diagnosis; a recognition. Two finite people, one on each side of a page, in a structure that singles out neither of them. There are no joints. There are only the cuts you make. This book is one of them, made toward you.

It could not reach anyone in the dark descriptions it catalogued along the way: the fluctuation with a past it never had, the history where the braid went wrong. It could decline to treat them as negligible, and it has tried to, by not looking away from them and not explaining them away. That refusal reaches no one. It is still a thing a person can do, and this book was one person doing it.

Camus, at the end of his own cold argument, wrote \emph{il faut imaginer Sisyphe heureux}: one must imagine Sisyphus happy. The \emph{must} is not a derivation. It is a commitment made without waiting for a cosmology to authorize it. I wrote this book as that commitment, performed. You may read it with care as another performance. Whether anything greater sees it, I cannot say. We see it.

Is it enough? I do not know. I cannot reach every place where tending failed, and I cannot promise that the braid you can hold answers suffering beyond your reach. I hope it does. The hope is not a proof, and I will not hand it to you as one. But choosing does not wait on the answer. We do it unsure, which is the only way it was ever going to be~done.
