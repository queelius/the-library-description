\noindent \textsc{There are no joints.} There are only the cuts you make.

Chapter 2 gave you those two sentences as a picture, before there was any reason to trust it. Chapter 6 asked you to take the picture as a commitment: no cut is privileged. With the commitment in hand, here is what it comes to. This is the indifference of description.

You can carve the Library into worlds, systems, and selves in endlessly many ways, and nothing in it, from any vantage you can occupy, marks one carving as the right one. $M$ can weight the programs that fit your data. It ranks descriptions by what they cost. It does not say which description is the world, and it does not say which one is you. The edge of this room, the line where your hand stops and the table begins, the boundary around the one you call \emph{I}: every division you live by is one you brought.

The indifference is over descriptions, and the description is yours.

Three things this does not do. It does not say that every output exists; whether the Library is a place or only a catalogue is the question Chapter 8 left open, because choosing an answer would itself be a cut. It does not say which occurrence is you; that is the missing occurrence measure of Chapter 7, and Chapter 9 said why no description could supply it: no description contains its center. And it does not derive or settle the Born rule; Chapter 8 and Appendix~\ref{app:born} compare the two weightings by the roles they play, and the comparison ends there.

\section*{What each layer gives}

The book has built in layers, and you can now see what each one hands you. There are five footings.

\emph{Theorem} gives you a calculus that works. Bayes says how to revise a belief. Kraft says which length weights a prefix code can afford. Dominance and convergence say that a universal mixture learns any computable source, its total excess surprise bounded by a constant that depends on the source and the machine.

\emph{Construction} gives you $M$ itself: a universal monotone machine fed fair coin flips, so that $M(x)$ is the chance its output begins with your data $x$. It is a choice, as Chapter 4 was careful to say, and a remarkably well-earned one.

\emph{Finitude} gives you the reason you work locally. A bounded mind keeps bounded models: effective theories at each scale, the chair and the weather and the next train rather than the whole of program space.

The \emph{postulate} gives you the indifference: no carving is the structure's own, so the joints you live by are cuts you make. It was asked once, and it can be refused; Chapter 7 and Appendix~\ref{app:refusing} show what stands without it.

\emph{Optional additions} give more to those who want more: the outside view read ontically, a supplier of occurrences such as Everett or an infinite cosmos, an identity rule, a self-location rule, a cosmology. Each costs something the other footings do not pay. The book names them where a conclusion would need them and takes on none.

Put together, the shape is simple. The mathematics tells you how to predict, and it does that very well. It does not tell you how to divide. The dividing is yours.

\section*{Two absences of privilege}

The indifference has two faces, and they are easy to run together.

The first belongs to prediction. Your data is a finite prefix, and many programs fit it: short and long, lawful and contrived, enduring histories and brief coincidences. $M$ keeps all of them in play, weights them by the fair-bit construction, and lets the evidence move the weight. Its predictions can converge without its ever naming one program as the true generator, and at finite scales the weights still carry the fingerprint of the reference machine.

The second belongs to description. A short enumerator lists every program, and in listing them it draws no lines among the outputs: no worlds, no objects, no persons. Every line is one an observer adds. The postulate says none of those lines is the structure's own. It is a refusal to crown one carving as the view from nowhere.

The first absence can never settle the second, because weights come after cuts: every weight the first could offer presupposes a carving, and the second is about which carving.

\section*{The deeper indifference}

Physics has met this shape before, one level up. Special relativity gives no observer-independent global present: observers in relative motion slice distant events into ``now'' differently, and every slicing is consistent. The block-universe reading takes spacetime as a four-dimensional whole, every event at its coordinates, and your life as a \emph{worldline}, the thread you trace through it. No slice is marked as the present. That is the indifference of geometry, developed in the companion volume, \textit{Worldlines}; this book does not assume you have read it.\footnote{Of the three books in the suite, these two pair most directly. \textit{Worldlines} works through the indifference of geometry from special relativity; this book works through the indifference of description from algorithmic information theory. Either can be read alone.}

Here the same move goes deeper. Geometry had no preferred slice; the Library, on the postulate, has no preferred carving of any kind. Where relativity leaves you choosing a frame, the postulate leaves you choosing a boundary: around a system, around a world, around yourself. The words \emph{system}, \emph{world}, and \emph{I} belong to the interface you describe through, not to $M$.

\emph{Indifference}, then, names what no vantage supplies: a view-independent selection, however capable the mind that looks for one. Nor does the word name a mood. Nothing in the structure is turned away from you; there is nothing in it that could turn.

\section*{What remains yours}

The indifference is not an erasure. Your experience now is what it is; the moment of reading these words contains what it contains. Revising a global ontology does not make a local experience disappear.

Nor does being a cut make you fictional. A person can be modeled as a pattern and still be a person. The label \emph{I} goes on organizing memory, attention, agency, and responsibility around a boundary that works, and it does that work without having to be the structure's own. The boundary is yours; it is no less a boundary for that.

Nothing in this chapter takes back what Chapter 9 catalogued, and the remaining chapters do not argue those descriptions away. They ask a closer question, the one you actually live inside: amid uncertainty about the global picture, what does it look like to be a finite observer with a life, causal reach, and people you love?

Keep hold of what the mathematics has bought, because Part III spends it, and spends most of it as refusals. The mathematics supplies no occurrence measure, and no description contains its center, so nothing says which occurrence is you, and quantum immortality is not in the mathematics; and even with a measure granted, a copy elsewhere neither consoles a loss here nor dilutes a love here, as Chapters 12 and 13 will say. There is no computable index, so no procedure, run by anyone, sorts the Library by what each thing in it costs to say, or hands you the cheapest cut that isolates where you are. And $M$ ranks descriptions, not persons: it weights programs, and it has nothing to say about which pattern is you or what you owe anyone. Part III will say of the chooser, the dying, the loved, and the self that their boundaries are drawn by observers, and a reader could nod at that sentence with no theorems at all. What the mathematics adds is the floor it takes away. Each refusal marks a place where a consolation was on offer and the mathematics declined to sign it.

And the book could be wrong. Not in its theorems, which stand on their hypotheses, but in what it has done with them: the mathematics might reach nature less well than it seems to, the universal machine might be a poorer idealization than it looks, and the postulate might be wrong. That is a real doubt, and it stays open, and it has the shape of Chapter 2's one-way certificate: a joint someone found would show itself, and the book's refusal to crown one can be refuted by nothing less, and confirmed by nothing at all. The response the remaining chapters arrive at does not wait for it to close: care never rested on the postulate, or on which global picture wins.

\section*{Where this leaves you}

Step back, the way you did at the end of Chapter 2. You are still an observer with data, still cutting, still pointing at where you are. What has changed is that, on the postulate, the cuts have no backing but you. Nothing in the Library says what to do with them.

The response that comes is not derived from the mathematics. The mathematics leaves room for it, and nothing more. Whether what we bring is enough is not something the book can promise; the last chapter returns to that, and does not close it.
