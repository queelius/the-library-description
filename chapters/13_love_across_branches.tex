\chapter[Love Across Branches]{Love\\Across Branches}

\chapterepigraph[0.6]
{For one human being to love another: that is perhaps the most difficult of all our tasks, the ultimate, the last test and proof, the work for which all other work is but preparation.}
{Rainer Maria Rilke, \textit{Letters to a Young Poet} (1929), trans.\ Herter Norton}

\noindent \textsc{Here is an image for love.} Two worldlines, two curves through spacetime, drawing close and winding around each other, each bending the other's path. A braid in the manifold. Not a metaphor for love but the geometry of it: where two lives shape each other, the structure itself carries a braid, and on the block-universe reading the braid is as real and as permanent as anything else at its coordinates.

This book takes the image and thickens it.

\section*{The braid, frayed}

You and the person you love can each be described as changing patterns rather than perfectly bounded substances. Related descriptions include histories in which you never met, met and parted, or remained together; on Everett's reading, or an ontic reading of Picture A, those histories would be more than descriptions. Figure~\ref{fig:braid} makes that conditional picture visible. The relationship here does not depend on settling it.

One reaction to this picture is that it cheapens the thing. If there were countless similar relations, would this one become a fraction of a total?

No. Chapter 12 said why a description elsewhere does not console a loss here, and the same reasoning keeps it from diluting a love here. Moral importance is not divided by a hypothetical copy count. The braid between you and the person in the room is not one-millionth of a braid if similar patterns are described elsewhere. Its importance arises in the lives it shapes here.

\section*{The braid that went wrong}

The honesty cuts the other way too, and the book will not pretend otherwise.

Picture A permits cruel descriptions as readily as kind ones, and Chapter 12 has already said what that symmetry is worth as comfort. The carelessness in your own past cannot be mended by imagining a kinder version elsewhere.

This is the cold the book keeps returning to. It does not offer multiplicity as a ledger in which kind possibilities cancel cruel ones. There is no cancellation. There is what happened here, and what you do next.

What you do next is the move you have, and it is worth making.

\section*{What is and is not your business}

Here the book asks for a discipline, and it is the one that runs under the whole of Part III.

Possible other braids are not within your causal reach, whether or not Picture A is read ontically. Moral action travels along actual connections: the person in the room, the next call, the unanswered message. You need not assert distant occurrences to refuse indifference toward possible suffering. Orientation can be broad; action remains local.

What this leaves you is small, and it is not nothing. The braid at these coordinates is what you have, and tending it is the whole of what is available. ``Enough'' here does not mean maximal, and it does not mean consoling. It means what is here, attended to. That is the only form care has ever taken, in any framework. The Library does not change what care is. It only removes the fantasy that there was ever more on offer than the coordinates you actually occupy. The braid is a cut you keep making; the structure keeps none of them for you.

\section*{Two at a table}

The abstraction has been doing a great deal of work, so set it down for a moment and look at the thing itself. A kitchen, late. The specific person across the table, holding a mug the way they always hold it, both hands, though the coffee went cold an hour ago. A conversation you have had before and may have again. None of it is underwritten.

And the mug is still in both hands. The sentence is still unfinished. The person holding the mug is this one and not an abstract class. That is a whole encounter, owing nothing to descriptions in which the chair is empty or occupied by someone else. This is the one your hands can touch.

This is what tending looks like with the metaphysics taken out: not a bond the cosmos maintains on your behalf, but two finite people in a room for a while. The acts stay concrete. You refill the mug. You finish the sentence kindly instead of sharply. You stay at the table a little past when you meant to leave.

\section*{We}

There is a word this chapter has been leaning on as though it were simple: \emph{we}.

Watch where its edge falls. At the table it holds two people. At a funeral it holds everyone who came, and some who could not. In a hospital corridor at three in the morning it can hold a stranger whose name you never learn, because the two of you are waiting for news from behind the same door. In a war it is drawn around a country, and drawn hard, so that a family on the far bank of a river is outside it. The word is the same each time. The line is not. Nothing in the people says where it goes, and nothing in the structure does. Someone draws it.

So \emph{we} is a cut, like the shelves in Borges' library. The labels \emph{you}, \emph{them}, and \emph{us} are models a finite observer runs. Nothing underwrites them.

It is easy to hear that as a demotion: if \emph{us} is drawn rather than found, it must be less real than it felt, a story two people have agreed to tell. But look at what the drawing does. The line around the table is how the table gets set: two mugs, not one, a second chair pulled out. The line around the corridor is why you hand the stranger the coffee you bought for yourself. At the scale where people live, the cut is not a veneer over some truer relation underneath. It is how the relation gets done.

This is also why the discipline earlier in the chapter, broad in orientation and local in action, is not a contradiction. You can draw \emph{we} as wide as you are willing to, wide enough that no one who suffers is left outside what you refuse to forget, and you will still set one table at a time. Neither line comes from the structure, so neither overrules the other. You draw both, for different jobs.

No global measure confers value on the staying; its value is in what it does to the people there. No global measure draws the line around them, either. Those lines are cuts.

We make them anyway.
