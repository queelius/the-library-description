\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\protect\numberline{}Preface}

This is a book about mathematics. It is also a book about why you should be gentle with the people around you. The connection between those two things is not obvious, and the book takes its whole length to draw it.

The mathematics is the algorithmic information theory of the 1960s: a few results about probability, description, and computation that have been settled for sixty years. The book takes a handful of them and follows them, through one inversion of intuition, to their conclusions. The arguments are not difficult. A willingness to follow a picture is enough; the formalism is there for those who want it, and can be skimmed by those who do not.

The inversion is this. Generating everything is trivial: the totality of all computable structures, the Library of every possible book, is produced by a one-line program. What is hard, what is the whole of the problem, is locating yourself within it. The index by which you would locate yourself, the system you call the world, even the self that does the locating: each is a cut you make in a structure that has none of its own. You are not a single observer with a single history. You are a pattern instantiated at many indices in the Library, in many systems, under many possible laws, and a measure weights all of it and singles out none of it. Not which index is you. Not which system is real. There is no privileged place to stand, and no privileged mind above it all keeping the count. There is only the structure, and the measure, and you, somewhere inside, unable to say exactly where.

This is the third of three books built the same way. \textit{Worldlines} took relativity literally and arrived at a four-dimensional structure indifferent to the lives threaded through it. \textit{Multitudes} took quantum mechanics literally and arrived at the same indifference one level deeper, in the measure on the branches. This book goes one level further out, to the measure on everything that can be computed, and finds the indifference there too: at the bottom, with nothing under it.

The structure is indifferent. We are not.

The structure does not care which index is yours, and there is no one above it who does. The only warmth the Library contains is the warmth we show each other inside it, here, at the coordinates we actually occupy, between finite patterns who will not meet again. The measure does not provide it. We provide it. We are all each other has.

Hello, fellow sufferer. Stay with me.
