\chapter*{A Note Before You Begin}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\protect\numberline{}A Note Before You Begin}

Some readers will come to this book at a hard moment. A few will arrive carrying an idea they met elsewhere: that if every possible version of a person exists, then one life is weightless or inescapable. This book does not establish that ontology or any first-person measure across versions. I want to say that before the mathematics, rather than leave it for a chapter you might not reach.

If you came hoping this book would give you permission to stop, it cannot. No theorem in these pages is a verdict on whether you should live. Put the global picture aside. What is here is smaller and more direct: one finite person writing to another and asking you to seek human help and stay.

You will encounter descriptions of indifference, uncertain identity, and painful possibilities. They are philosophical possibilities, not your address and not a reason to go. The book's chosen response is local: make contact with another person, accept care, and let the immediate moment become safer.

If you are in that hard moment now, turn to \textit{Resources}. If you may act soon or cannot stay safe, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department, and ask another person to stay with you. Please use the help there now.
