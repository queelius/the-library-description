\chapter*{A Note Before You Begin}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\protect\numberline{}A Note Before You Begin}

Some readers will come to this book at a hard moment. If that is you, and you may act on thoughts of suicide soon or cannot keep yourself safe, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department now, and, if you can, ask another person to stay with you. In the United States, call or text 988. In Canada, call or text 9-8-8. In the United Kingdom and Ireland, call Samaritans on 116 123. Anywhere, \url{https://findahelpline.com} can help you find a local service. \hyperref[resources]{\textit{Resources}, at the back of the book}, has the same help in full.

A few readers will arrive carrying an idea they met elsewhere: that if every possible version of a person exists, then one life is weightless or inescapable. Whatever turns out to be true about the universe, that idea is not a verdict on you, and this book does not argue for it. I want to say that before the mathematics, rather than leave it for a chapter you might not reach.

If you came hoping this book would give you permission to stop, it cannot. Nothing in these pages gives that permission: not the mathematics, not the structure. Put the global picture aside. What is here is smaller and more direct: one finite person writing to another and asking you to seek human help and stay.

You will encounter descriptions of indifference, uncertain identity, and painful possibilities. They are philosophical possibilities, not your address and not a reason to go. The book's chosen response is local: make contact with another person, accept care, and let the immediate moment become safer.
