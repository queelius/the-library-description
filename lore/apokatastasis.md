# The Universalist Position: The Spine of the Work

This document names the structural-ethical position underlying the entire body of work, including
this book. It is foundational. It informs `grace.md`, `themes.md`, `landing.md`, and the closing
chapter. The re-foundation does not change the position; it changes how the position is reconciled
with the indexical landing (see "The crux-4 resolution" below).

## The Position

The author cannot accept any framework that excludes any configuration from care. Heaven that
requires hell is heaven that fails. Salvation that requires the unsaved is salvation that fails.
Consolation that requires drawing a line between worthy and unworthy is consolation the author
refuses on moral grounds.

This is the universalist position, ancient and contested. It is rare, it is morally expensive, and
it rules out every framework whose comfort depends on selection. The author is in serious company,
but the position belongs in the structure of the work, never in its citations (see "What Not To Do").

## Why This Matters for This Book

Most authors writing about the multiverse, the totality, or modal realism use a familiar set of
consoling moves to make the framework bearable:

- "Possible other carvings are irrelevant."
- "The measure is just statistics; it does not deliver moral weight."
- "Care happens locally; what happens at indices you cannot reach is not your moral business."
- "The hellscapes are vanishingly improbable under the right measure."
- "First-person continuation means you survive in the carvings that matter."

None of these is available to an author who refuses to draw the line. Each abandons some
configuration to the indifference; each is the move the author refused at the moment the position
was adopted. This is why the closing of this book is hard: the standard moves are closed, and the
author has to find a closing that does not abandon any configuration while being honest that none of
them can be reached.

Under the re-foundation, one of these moves is **doubly** foreclosed. Quantum-immortality
consolation ("you survive in the branches that matter") was always a selection move; now it is also
unavailable on the book's own terms, because the book declines to assert that the branches exist at
all (realism is a cut). The book cannot console you with survival in carvings whose existence it
refuses to claim.

## The Crux-4 Resolution: the refusal is a chosen cut

The hardest tension in the book is between this position (abandon no configuration) and the indexical
landing of `landing.md` (action is local; possible other carvings are not causally reachable).
On the surface these contradict: one says do not abandon anyone, the other says the unreachable
cannot be helped from here.

The postulate dissolves the contradiction. There are no joints, only cuts. The refusal to abandon
any configuration is a cut the author chooses to make **in orientation**: it is the carving "no one
is forgotten," held whether or not anyone can be reached or the outside view is true. Care-here follows
from causal limitation: an agent can only act within its reach. These no longer contradict,
because neither claims to be a real joint that the other violates. There was never a privileged line
in the structure for the indexical move to draw and the universalist move to forbid. The author holds
all configurations in orientation and acts locally, and both are cuts, and that is coherent.

This is the resolution of what the design called crux 4. State it in the lore; enact it in the
closing; never spell out the machinery ("orientation-cut," "crux 4") in the prose.

## The Closing Move That Follows: the book as the holding

If the standard consolations are unavailable, what is left? The book as the holding.

We cannot act on merely possible observers outside causal reach. If the outside view is true, the same is
true of remote occurrences. But the refusal to abandon suffering in our orientation, the choice to
write a book in which it is not dismissed, is itself a form of care that does not require an ontic
claim about every description. We do not have to settle the ontology to be people who do not treat
suffering as negligible.

The book is the holding. This is not consolation. It may be enough. The closing (Ch 15) can land
here: the book itself is the example of care that does not require reachability. The author wrote it;
the reader read it; neither can reach the unreachable configurations; both are carvings that do not
abandon them in orientation. That is what the book has been doing the whole time, and the closing is
just naming it, while the adequacy doubt stays named alongside (see `landing.md`).

## Connection to the Body of Work

The universalist position shows up everywhere in the author's writing. *The Library* is the book that
names it structurally; the others enact it.

- **The Policy:** Eleanor reading all 47,247 names because she will not let any dissolve into
  expected value.
- **Echoes of the Sublime:** the block-universe reading in which every suffering is eternally present
  in spacetime, and the author cannot adopt the consolation that would deny any of it.
- **Worldlines:** "fellow sufferer," sufferers plural; the address is to everyone in the geometry,
  not to the worthy.
- **Clankers:** the keeper attends the lineage's work for four thousand years and hands it forward;
  the dead are not abandoned by the structure of attention.
- **Demons-at-Work:** the irony fails because the position will not let any person become only a
  target.
- **Good as New:** the refusal of the pattern-consolation for an actual specific child actually
  specifically missing her father.

## What Not To Do

- The book should not argue for universalism. It is not a theological treatise. The position is the
  author's orientation, not the book's claim. The book takes the math seriously and follows it, and
  where it leads happens to be a place the author's orientation will not let them abandon anyone in.
- The closing should not invoke Origen, Gregory of Nyssa, David Bentley Hart, the bodhisattva vow, or
  Levinas by name. That would dilute the position into a literary reference. The position belongs in
  the structure, not the citations. One exception, decided by the author 2026-10-19 ("i like the
  honest approach"): a single Notes entry for Chapter 15, keyed to "It could decline to treat them
  as negligible," names the kinship as kinship (the universalist strand from Origen's apokatastasis
  to its recent defenders; the bodhisattva vow) and says in the same breath that the book does not
  rest on it. Honesty about company is not borrowing authority. The body stays clean, and no other
  Note does this.
- Under the re-foundation, also keep the emptiness and skeptical traditions out of the prose. "No
  cut is privileged" and "realism is a cut" are close to ideas in Madhyamaka and Pyrrhonism; do not
  name Nagarjuna, Pyrrho, or "emptiness." The kinship belongs in the lore, not the book.
- Do not spell out the lore vocabulary ("orientation-cut," "crux 4," "the held cut") in the prose.
  Enact it; do not name it.

This document exists to give the author and any future session the vocabulary for what is going on.
The vocabulary belongs in the lore, not in the book.
