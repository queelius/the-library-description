# Information Hazards: Analysis and Mitigations

This document captures whether the book could harm vulnerable readers, what specifically is
hazardous, what mitigations are in place, and what to add before publication. It has been
re-audited for the re-foundation: the postulate ("no cut is privileged") and the (c) ontology
("realism is a cut") change the hazard profile in two directions, lowering one risk and raising
another.

## The Question

Is publishing this book a net harm because it transmits ideas that could destabilize vulnerable
readers? The honest answer: there is real but bounded risk, smaller than the help the book will
provide most readers, and reducible by specific craft choices. The book should publish, with care.

## What is Actually Hazardous

Ranked roughly by severity, re-audited under the re-foundation.

1. **Groundlessness and derealization (raised by the re-foundation).** This is now the sharpest
   risk. The old framing asserted a real multiverse the reader could at least stand inside; the new
   framing says no cut is privileged, realism is itself a cut, and the self is a cut. "There is no
   fact of the matter about what is real, including you" can trigger or deepen depersonalization and
   derealization in vulnerable readers more readily than the older, more furnished picture did. The
   self-dissolution that Parfit's milder version has destabilized philosophy students with for forty
   years is here taken one level further: not "the self is impermanent" but "the self is a boundary
   you drew." This is the move most worth handling with care in the prose.

2. **The permanence-of-suffering-across-carvings implication.** The block-universe S-risk reframed
   as nonfiction, in the dark reading a reader may take away even though the book does not assert
   it: "your worst moment exists at its coordinates in countless carvings, and the structure ranks
   none of them." For a reader with chronic depression, trauma, or active
   suicidality, this lands wrong and lingers. Distilled from the horror register of Echoes into the
   author's nonfiction voice, it is a different kind of transmission.

3. **The total cold of "the structure does not notice," now with no overseer at all.** "No view from
   the top" makes the indifference not just unpreferring but unwatched, and not contingently. For a
   reader already feeling invisible, this can confirm a depressive worldview in a register that feels
   mathematically supported.

4. **The Boltzmann shadow, reframed.** "Your past may not be real" becomes "the past is a cut." The
   reframing is intellectually honest but does not help a reader already in derealization, and it
   compounds risk 1.

5. **Quantum immortality (lowered by the re-foundation).** This was the second-worst risk in the old
   draft (the "I cannot even escape by dying" intrusion). Under the re-foundation it is **demoted**:
   the book no longer asserts the branches are real (realism is a cut), adopts no indexical
   assumption, and explicitly refuses to privilege the carving in which you continue. The distressing
   claim is now one the book declines to make, named as a carving the reader is not required to
   inhabit. It remains worth handling carefully, but it is no longer load-bearing and no longer
   asserted.

## What is Not Hazardous

The mathematics itself. Bayes, Kraft, Solomonoff have been in plain sight since the 1960s; anyone
harmed by the mathematics could already have been harmed by Hutter, the Wikipedia article on
Solomonoff induction, Tegmark, the SEP entry on the branching interpretation, or Bostrom's
simulation paper. The book does not reveal the mathematics; it synthesizes. The synthesis, the
cascade from bedrock to the indifference of description, is the author's contribution, and it lands
at care, which most existing literature in this space does not. The book is structurally gentler than
what it draws from.

## Counterfactual

A reader who would be harmed by this book and reads something else instead may read worse: Cioran,
Ligotti, Benatar, or the online multiverse, simulation, and Boltzmann communities where the material
arrives without the author's care. There is a population actively looking for this framework; they
will find something. Withholding the book is not neutral; it is a decision to let cruder versions
have the field.

## Mitigations Already in Place

- Care is the explicit destination, named and structurally central (the held cut).
- The one interpretive commitment, the postulate, is labeled where it enters and may be refused; the
  reader is never asked to take the ontology on faith.
- The voice is companionate from the first sentence of the preface.
- Worldlines (the companion) establishes the same direction more gently and can be read first.
- The book is short and does not dwell.

These are the reason the book is publishable.

## Additional Mitigations to Consider

### A direct address near the front to the reader who came in crisis

Not a pathologizing warning. A sentence or two in the author's voice acknowledging that some readers
come at a hard moment, and naming what the book wants for them. Candidate framing:

> If you came to this book hoping it would give you permission to stop, I want to give you the
> opposite. The math does not give permission. The structure does not give permission. The
> permission you are looking for is not in the Library. What is in the Library is this configuration:
> a finite primate writing to another finite primate, asking them to stay.

It can sit at the end of the preface or as a short note after it. As published, the front crisis
note now **opens with the emergency route and contacts** (immediate danger first, then the
services), links to Resources, and only then speaks in the author's voice. It does not answer the
misconception with ontology (no "this book does not establish that ontology"); the footing it
offers is local and practical.

### Resources at the end

The Resources back-matter must route action in this order, which matches the published page:

1. **Immediate danger.** If the reader may act soon or is in immediate physical danger, call the
   local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department, and ask another person to
   stay with them.
2. **The disclosure script.** One plain sentence the reader can say or text: "I am thinking about
   suicide and need help staying safe."
3. **Services.** United States: 988 (call or text). Canada: 9-8-8 (call or text). United Kingdom
   and Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123 (free, 24/7). Elsewhere: findahelpline.com.

The copy must be unconditional and non-metaphysical. Do not argue from the Library, tell the reader
what their continued life means to loved ones, or turn staying alive into an obligation. The purpose
of the page is immediate connection to human help.

### Calibrated framing on the sharpest moves

In the groundlessness, permanence-of-suffering, and Boltzmann passages, add one sentence each that
names the response to the dark version. Not a comfort: a note that the framework permits this reading
and does not require the reader to take it as their reality, that many readers will choose not to,
and that the cascade survives the refusal. Chapter 9 already carries a calibrated reader-safety note;
tighten it at the sharpest points.

### The groundlessness register specifically (new)

Because the re-foundation raises the derealization risk (risk 1), give it the same treatment the old
draft reserved for quantum immortality. Near the self-dissolution (Ch 14) and the limits (Ch 9),
state plainly that "the self is a cut" is a claim about a model and boundary, not a claim that a
person or an experience is unreal. The footing the reader can stand on is local and practical:
whatever the global ontology, this person is here, effects are felt here, and care changes what
happens here. Do not recruit the postulate or a metaphysical claim as crisis intervention.

### Quantum immortality (now lower priority)

Under the re-foundation this is no longer the passage most worth revisiting; the demotion has already
done most of the work. Keep it as a labeled implication the book declines to assert, with the
reader-safety note, and do not give it a full load-bearing section. The author's personal reasons for
caution and the book's structural reasons not to need it still point the same way.

"The reader-safety note" means a note in **Chapter 11 itself**, where quantum immortality is named,
not only Chapter 9's general note. Chapter 11 states the idea plainly and labeled, and carries one
plain, non-metaphysical sentence in the body: the thought experiment is not a reason to take any
real risk, with a pointer to Resources. Its examples of choices the framework does not rank are
ordinary (a job, a phone call), never a lethal act, and the reader is never cast, in the second
person, as the one killed. The detailed premise analysis lives in Appendix C, which keeps the same
framing. The Notes entry on Tegmark's "quantum suicide" thought experiment carries the same
caution.

### Safety notes live in the body

Every safety note is body text, never a footnote. The Chapter 12 safety note moves from a footnote
into the body, matching Chapter 9's. No safety note assumes the reader has someone to turn to;
each can point to Resources as the unconditional route.

## The Author's Standing

The question "would publishing this do harm?" is one the author should keep asking, and the asking is
the qualification for being the author of this book. An author who would publish without considering
harm is the author who should not write it; an author who agonizes over it is the author who should.
The risk does not become zero by publishing carefully; it becomes manageable, which is the most that
can be asked of any serious book about anything hard.
