# Outline

*The Library: The Indifference of Description.* Three parts, fifteen chapters. The three parts
are the one move (the cut, equivalently local compression) at three depths: you cut the totality
to **predict** (The Index), to have a **world** (The System), and to be **someone** (The Self).
The chapter order is unchanged from the prior draft; the parts name what was already there.
Target length: 20,000 to 25,000 words.

The reasoning skeleton (see `foundations.md`) is bedrock (settled math) to the postulate ("no
cut is privileged") to finitude (a consequence of Kraft) to consequences. Every chapter's
argument is traceable to one of those.

---

## Part I: The Index (the cut for prediction)

*The reader meets the mathematics through ordinary observation, arrives at the universal
distribution by Chapter 4, sees the inversion in Chapter 2, and learns that to predict is to
cut. The index is the first instance of the move.*

### Chapter 1: The Question

You wake up. You observe data. You predict. Everyday induction: the sun has risen every day, so
you expect it tomorrow. Why is the prediction reasonable? The naive answer (uniformity of
nature) is interrogated and left open. **The cut, named:** prediction is already a restriction,
a slice taken from what could be.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Russell on the chicken that knew, with great confidence, that food
would come every morning.

### Chapter 2: The Library

The inversion. The generator for the totality is one line; the Kolmogorov complexity of
"everything" is small; the hard problem is locating *this*. Borges' Library of Babel; the
empty/universal duality; "the information lives in the cut." **The cut, named explicitly here
for the first time, with its native sentence.** A short "note on the metaphor" flags that the
shelves are the librarian's imposition and points forward to Chapter 14. The reader leaves with
an inversion they cannot un-think: there are no joints, only the cuts you make.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Borges, "The Library of Babel."

### Chapter 3: The Indexing

Bayes. The structure of belief revision; Cox's uniqueness. We have been cutting all along, using
Bayesian inference informally: the prior is the cut before data, the posterior the cut after.
Bayes does not say which prior to use; that silence is the opening Chapter 4 fills. **The cut,
named:** indexing is conditioning, the verb sense of the cut.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Jaynes, on probability as extended logic.

### Chapter 4: The Prior

Kraft's inequality; prefix-free codes turn length into probability; the universal prior
$M(x) = \sum 2^{-|p|}$; $K(x)$ as the shortest description. The simplicity bias was mathematical
structure all along. **The cut, and finitude:** this is the natural home for the guardrail. The
prior is not the structure's preference; a finite cutter is *forced* toward cheap cuts because
Kraft makes short descriptions scarce. State this plainly here.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Solomonoff on universal induction, or Chaitin on randomness.

### Chapter 5: The Optimality

Solomonoff's dominance theorem: the universal prior catches up to any computable
distribution. The invariance theorem: the choice of machine is asymptotically irrelevant. The
math of prediction is now closed. **The cut:** optimality is for prediction, and it is what
finitude buys; reiterate that the bias is forced on the observer, never in the structure (hedge
any scaling-law analogy as an echo, not a proof). The chapter ends by turning toward reality: what
happens when we read this not as how to learn but as what is?

**Epigraph (candidate):** Hutter, on the universal prior dominating every computable
distribution up to a constant.

## Part II: The System (the cut for a world)

*The same Bayesian structure can be read as a measure on hypotheses or as a claim about reality.
The slip into ontology is a cut, the second instance of the move. The reader meets the one
postulate, the groundlessness, and the indifference.*

### Chapter 6: The Slip

The pivot. The same structure reads epistemically (a measure on hypotheses) or ontologically (a
measure on realities), and the interpretation is the cut. **The postulate enters here:** no cut
is privileged. "Only this one world is real" is shown to be a cut that costs information;
declining to privilege it is what is left when you stop carving. The old two-move framing is
gone; there is one postulate, asked once.

**Epigraph:** Wheeler, "It from bit."

### Chapter 7: The Inhabited Library

The picture the slip gives, the old question it sharpens (why is there something rather than
nothing), and the humility hinge (what survives if you refuse the postulate). "Where am I?"
becomes a question without a privileged answer. The nothing question is taken seriously and not
pretended-resolved (nothing is itself a cheap cut; observation excludes it; coherence is
flagged). A note on time: computation is static, time is a logical index, the same eternalism as
the block universe. **The cut:** the inhabited world is a carving; contingency is relocated, not
resolved.

**Epigraph:** Wittgenstein, "The world is everything that is the case."

### Chapter 8: The Observer

Quantum mechanics as a familiar landmark, not a route: the plainest reading of the unmodified
Schrodinger equation already branches, shown briefly and cross-referenced to *Multitudes* for the
full treatment. The Born-measure / universal-distribution identification is made here: both are
the bookkeeping of cuts, the seam to *Multitudes*. Then the chapter defines what an observer is:
a local substructure doing local inference, with hierarchical decomposability as the condition
for inference to work, and the self as a short local program. "Where am I?" becomes sharp; SSA,
SIA, and the speed prior are noted as none-forced and adopted by none. **The cut:** the observer
is itself a carving; realism is a cut (Picture A vs Picture B, and choosing between them is a
cut).

**Epigraph:** Bryce DeWitt, on every quantum transition splitting the local world into myriads
of copies of itself (1970).

### Chapter 9: The Limits

The close of Part II and its coldest move. First, **no view from the top:** no view is privileged
because every view is a cut and no cut is privileged, so there is no overseer, and not
contingently; what is above all minds is the structure, which is not a mind and surveys nothing.
This follows from the postulate, not from a strained argument about the limits of computation. The
grace of Part III is seeded here (warmth, if any, is among the rungs, not handed down). Then the
catalogue of what the framework permits: Boltzmann observers, hellscapes, dark continuation, with
a calibrated reader-safety note (the framework permits this; it does not place you in one; you may
decline to live inside it). Then "what if you refuse the postulate": the bedrock survives any
refusal.

**Epigraph:** Spinoza, "Nature has no particular goal in view, and final causes are but human
figments." (*Ethics*, 1677)

### Chapter 10: The Indifference of Description

The titular chapter and the cap of The System. The thesis: the structure ranks carvings by cost
and singles out none, neither which index is you nor which system is real nor which boundary is
the self. The full picture is now in view; it cascades through Part III. **The cut:** the
indifference is over descriptions, and the description is yours.

This is the analog of Worldlines' "The Block Universe" chapter, one level deeper.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Whitman, "I am large, I contain multitudes."

## Part III: The Self (the cut for being someone, and the response)

*The bedrock, the postulate, and the indifference are in place. Now: what does the cut do to the
human concerns, and what is the chosen response? The self is the third and deepest instance of
the move, and care is the cut we choose.*

### Chapter 11: The Question of Continuation

Free will and first-person continuation. The compatibilist response refined by branching: you
choose, the choice happens across branches, the felt openness and the structural multiplicity
coexist. Quantum immortality introduced as a labeled implication, not a settled result; the
asymmetry between first-person continuation and third-person observation. **The cut:** the chooser
is a carving.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Spinoza on freedom and causation, or a contemporary philosopher on
indexical reasoning.

### Chapter 12: The Dissolution of Death

Mortality. Worldlines treated death as the boundary of the worldline; this book refines it to an
event with first-person and third-person components, and sits with the asymmetry between
continuation (where it exists) and loss (where it is real) without resolving it. It does not claim
quantum immortality consoles. **The cut:** the boundary called death is a carving; what the living
lose is real at their coordinates.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Mary Oliver on darkness and gift, or Epicurus on death and
non-experience, transformed.

### Chapter 13: Love Across Branches

Love and grief. The braid extended: two patterns intertwined across many carvings, each
instantiation real at its coordinates. The compassion argument is deepened, not weakened, by
multiplicity; the book acknowledges that carvings contain cruelty too, and the chosen response is
to act here for care. Holds the concrete object (a table, late; a mug held both-handed; the cold
coffee). **The cut:** the braid is a carving we maintain; "we" is a cut we make anyway.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Rilke, *Letters to a Young Poet*.

### Chapter 14: The Pattern

The self. Worldlines dissolved the self temporally; this book dissolves it as a carving. You are a
short local program, a boundary drawn around a region for navigation, and so is everyone you love.
Self and index dissolve together: one move at two levels. Extends Parfit (identity versus what
matters): identity is a cut, but what matters is real wherever it is instantiated. **The cut, all
the way down:** this is the convergence the whole book has built toward.

**Epigraph (candidate):** Hume, *Treatise*, on the self as a bundle of perceptions.

### Chapter 15: The Pattern Describing Itself

Closing. The book is one carving in the totality; reading it is another; care is another. The held
cut: warmth between cuts, the book itself the holding performed. The Worldlines rhythm (rage at the
indifference, the rage passes, equanimity, care remains), with the adequacy-doubt named: the
closing asks whether care is enough, answers "I do not know," names the hope as hope, and chooses
unsure. A header-less coda; the address is the single word "Stay."; Camus's *must* is literal.

**Epigraph (candidate):** George Eliot, *Middlemarch* finale, on unvisited tombs.

## Pedagogical sequencing

Each step is set up by the previous, tracing the new skeleton:

1. **Induction is universal** (Ch 1). Everyone predicts; prediction is already a cut.
2. **The totality has no joints** (Ch 2). Generation is trivial; the cut is everything.
3. **Bayes is the cut for prediction** (Ch 3). Uniquely consistent.
4. **Kraft gives the prior, finitude forces it** (Ch 4). Short descriptions are scarce.
5. **Solomonoff is optimal for prediction** (Ch 5). The bias is forced on the observer.
6. **The slip is a cut; the postulate** (Ch 6). No cut is privileged.
7. **The inhabited world; the nothing question; humility** (Ch 7). Contingency relocated.
8. **The observer defined; realism is a cut** (Ch 8). QM as landmark; the Born seam.
9. **The limits** (Ch 9). No view from the top; what the framework permits; refusal survives.
10. **The indifference of description** (Ch 10). Thesis stated; The System capped.
11. **Continuation** (Ch 11). The chooser is a cut.
12. **Death** (Ch 12). First-person continuation versus third-person loss.
13. **Love** (Ch 13). The braid as a carving we maintain.
14. **Self** (Ch 14). The cut all the way down; the convergence.
15. **Closing** (Ch 15). The held cut.
